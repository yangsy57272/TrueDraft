# TrueDraft formative usability test

Status: **protocol ready; participants not recruited and sessions not conducted.** No testimonials, completion rates, satisfaction scores, or user outcomes are implied.

## Aim and participants

Recruit five people from one intended user group, for example students preparing undergraduate applications. If counselors are a separate target group, study them separately. Use the fictional demo first; do not ask participants to disclose personal essays, grades, finances or application documents. Participation is voluntary, and they may stop at any point.

Suggested session: 20 minutes. Explain that the product is being tested, not the participant. Ask permission before recording; without permission, take minimal anonymous notes. Agree a deletion date for notes or recordings. Do not publish identities, screenshots or quotations without separate explicit consent. Do not send invitations automatically.

## Tasks (give goals, not button-by-button instructions)

1. Use the public demo to choose two colleges and explain why one was grouped as Reach or Target.
2. Find the next piece of work to do. Explain whether its target is an official deadline and whether time is estimated.
3. Find the evidence behind one essay comment and compare the illustrative revision. Explain whether a live AI produced it.
4. Find what the quality checks measure and one thing they do not measure.
5. Locate the correct desktop download. In an optional desktop session, create a fictional draft, restart, find it again, and locate export and backup instructions.

Observe without guiding. If stuck, ask “What are you looking for?” Record help provided. Stop tasks that would involve payment, credentials to an external service, or application submission.

## Debrief and reporting

Ask what was useful, confusing, and whether they would return for an actual application task. Do not ask for a positive review or a star as a condition of participation.

Report findings as observations from a small formative sample, identifying the build, tasks, recruitment method and limitations. Example format: “Two of five participants needed help locating X in this session”; include actual records before writing any count. Fix the largest obstacles, then repeat relevant tasks with new participants. Never convert these observations into admissions claims or an overall product accuracy rate.
