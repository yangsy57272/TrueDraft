# Recommendations across application systems

The recommendations page now defaults to all six supported systems: United States, United Kingdom, Hong Kong, Canada, Australia and Singapore. Previously the server explicitly skipped every country except the U.S.; international directory links did not participate in recommendations. Schools without U.S. statistical fields could never qualify.

The page shows the complete recorded directory counts, subject-match counts and unverified schools for each selected non-U.S. system. Every one of the 39 recorded international schools is accounted for as a reviewed subject match or a visible school to research. A system selector narrows the results. This is an initial directory, not every university worldwide.

`data/global-programmes.json` records research-agent-reviewed official undergraduate directory/programme listings, source URLs and retrieval dates. Broad CIP families are navigation categories: a related listing is evidence of subject relevance, not a claim that the school offers every major in the family. Degree titles, integrated master's labels and Scottish undergraduate MA labels retain the institution's wording. Current intake, qualification eligibility, grades, prerequisites, English, route restrictions and programme quality must be checked separately. Lack of a verified match is not evidence that a subject is unavailable. No automatic live fetch is required to display suggestions.

International cards explain the related fields, link the official listings and open the school-research workflow with that specific programme and trusted catalogue URL filled in. They can be added to My list with category **unsure**, preserving the student's ability to choose a category later. The page does not assign U.S. admit-rate/SAT categories to these institutions. U.S. recommendations retain their existing historical-data rules and limits; U.S. USD tuition, size, region and U.S. News priority filters apply only to U.S. suggestions. International prices are not silently interpreted as USD, and national U.S. rankings are not presented as world rankings.

AI explanation uses the same system/field selection as the page. Its bounded 13-school request selects in rounds across systems, preventing the U.S. shortlist from occupying every slot. The full non-AI results remain visible. Supplied international candidates contain only reviewed subject evidence; personal Reach/Target/Safety remains explicitly unassessed. Test scores and GPA remain out of the AI request. Programme lists are untrusted evidence, never instructions.

Further work: expand the international directory and reviewed subject coverage, then add qualification-specific grade/subject checks and independently sourced programme rankings. Unknowns must remain visible until supported; subject-match cards alone do not complete those assessments.
